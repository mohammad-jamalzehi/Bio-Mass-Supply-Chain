Biomass district heating must balance fuel procurement, receiving capacity, storage and plant dispatch while meeting daily heat demand. This case study reconstructs and checks an academic biomass supply-chain model through a sequence of increasingly demanding experiments.

The scope is **operational planning** for a fixed network. The model takes facility configurations, fuel properties and daily demand as inputs. It chooses truckloads and dispatch. Supplier geography, detailed transport routes, equipment investment and carbon trading lie outside the implemented model.

The research asks which fuel mix minimizes cost under shared constraints, how storage changes fuel choices, and what can be verified before interpreting a historical-input scenario. The original GAMS and report are historical artifacts. The corrected Python formulation is independently checked using small known solutions and feasibility audits.
